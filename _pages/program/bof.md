## Sunday, October 25, 2026

### Embeddings, Rerankers, Small Language Models for Better Search
**Time:** 11:00–12:30 (Session 2)  
**Location:** G9-10, G11  
**Organizer:** Han Xiao

**Description:**  
Embeddings and rerankers are the backbone of agentic search. These models are smaller and more discriminative than their large generative counterparts, but getting them to perform well still takes real expertise. After our Embeddings BoF at EMNLP 2023, 2024 and 2025, we've watched omnimodal embeddings, rerankers and general-purpose discriminative models like Jev take off. Come hear invited talks on what's new, debate where the research goes next, and talk through advance capabilities of those small models.

This is our fourth BoF on the same topic. Singapore, Miami, Suzhou: full house every time. With the hype around Jev, a general-purpose discriminative model, we expect even bigger turnout this year, and a wider conversation covering embeddings, rerankers and uses well beyond traditional search.

### Culturally Grounded and Trustworthy NLP: Perspectives from Islamic Media Ethics
**Time:** 16:00–17:30 (Session 4)  
**Location:** G9-10  
**Organizer:** Firoj Alam

**Description:**  
How should NLP systems respond when reliable information, cultural context, and ethical judgment intersect? This Birds-of-a-Feather session brings together researchers and practitioners interested in culturally grounded NLP, agentic AI, misinformation, trustworthy question answering, and responsible AI, drawing on perspectives from Islamic media ethics. The discussion will examine how agentic approaches, including retrieval-augmented generation and tool-assisted verification, can support evidence-grounded responses. We will explore their opportunities and limitations, particularly in handling insufficient evidence, conflicting sources, source attribution, and human oversight. We will also consider how ethical principles, including truthfulness, responsibility, justice, and avoidance of harm, can inform dataset design, model development, and evaluation. Discussions will distinguish factual verification from ethical and interpretive judgments, while recognizing diversity within and across religious and cultural traditions. Participants will explore three questions: When do agentic approaches improve the reliability of culturally grounded responses? How should systems represent uncertainty and differing interpretations? How can researchers, domain scholars, and communities jointly develop meaningful benchmarks and evaluation practices? Our goal is to identify shared research priorities, resource needs, and opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration. Participants from all backgrounds and all career stages are welcome.

This session aligns with EMNLP 2026’s theme, “New Missions for NLP Research,” by connecting culturally grounded evaluation, agentic AI, and responsible NLP. Drawing on Islamic media ethics, it explores evidence grounding, uncertainty, and diverse interpretations while encouraging collaboration among NLP researchers, domain scholars, and communities.

### Doing NLP Research in Japan
**Time:** 16:00–17:30 (Session 4)  
**Location:** G12  
**Organizer:** Benjamin Heinzerling

**Description:**  
Researchers currently working in Japan, researchers interested in working or studying in Japan, people collaborating with Japanese institutions, and anyone interested in strengthening connections between the Japanese and international NLP communities.

The EMNLP 2026 theme asks what NLP research should focus on now that strong general-purpose language models are widely available. Japan offers an interesting case: it has a large NLP community and substantial investment in language models, but is not currently home to a leading frontier-model developer. If building ever more capable general-purpose models is no longer the obvious mission of NLP, what should researchers in Japan focus on instead? This BoF will use Japan as a starting point for discussing new missions for NLP research.

### Trustworthy Language Model Agents for High-Stakes Industry Applications
**Time:** 09:00–10:30 (Session 5)  
**Location:** G9-10  
**Organizer:** Yuqicheng Zhu

**Description:**  
Language model agents are moving from demos into settings where a wrong action can be costly: suggesting a clinical intervention, modifying an industrial process, triggering an enterprise workflow, or making a high-impact recommendation. But what would it actually take to trust such an agent? The session will focus on two closely connected questions. First, what capabilities and safeguards are needed for trustworthy deployment in high-stakes applications? Second, which technical approaches are most promising for achieving these properties beyond controlled benchmark settings and in real-world deployment? We expect the session to foster an open exchange between researchers and practitioners from academia and industry, bringing together different perspectives on trustworthy language model agents in high-stakes applications. Beyond the session itself, we hope to identify shared challenges, stimulate new collaborations, and help build a community around trustworthy deployment of language model agents in real-world settings.

This session directly connects to the EMNLP 2026 theme, New Missions for NLP Research, by focusing on the transition from language models as benchmarked components to agentic systems deployed in consequential real-world settings. As language model agents increasingly interact with tools, environments, and human decision-making processes, progress can no longer be measured only through static benchmark performance. The session therefore emphasizes trustworthiness, robustness, and real-world deployment, and asks what new capabilities, safeguards, and technical approaches are needed for NLP systems operating in high-stakes industry applications.

### Linguistic Style across the NLP pipeline
**Time:** 16:45–18:15 (Session 9)  
**Location:** G12  
**Organizers:** Anna Wegmann, Hongyu Chen, Sarubi Thillainathan, JongKyung Shin, Christoph Leiter, Steffen Eger, Adrian de Wynter

**Description:**  
Style plays a central role across the full NLP pipeline: the stylistic diversity of training data influences model robustness, users expect generated texts in a helpful or personalized style, evaluation on texts with limited stylistic variation can obfuscate a model’s ability to function in the wild, and applications like writing assistance, AI-generated text detection, anonymization and authorship verification rely on style features. With this BoF session we want to connect researchers that are interested in how style can be modeled, measured and applied across the NLP pipeline. We will open the session with a 15-minute introduction to the topic and open questions, and will then encourage informal discussions around topics participants are interested in. They might cover style in language modeling and generation (personalization, style steering/transfer), measuring and explaining style, applications (authorship attribution and verification, AI-text detection, anonymization, forensic and literary analysis), and linguistic perspectives on style.

Style is central to New Missions for NLP Research: Language models encounter diverse registers, dialects and individual writing styles, and generate texts with stylistic prints of their own. Current benchmarks may capture only a narrow range of stylistic features or definitions of style, leaving open how to model and evaluate style across domains, tasks and social contexts. These questions are also relevant to applications such as writing assistance, personalization and chatbots, where style also depends on humans’ subjective preferences. Studying linguistic style thus contributes both to understanding human language and to developing language technologies that support diverse needs.

## Tuesday, October 27, 2026

### Data to Output: Diversity Across the NLP Pipeline
**Time:** 09:00–10:30 (Session 11)  
**Location:** G11  
**Organizer:** Cantao Su

**Description:**  
Diversity is increasingly discussed across the NLP pipeline, yet many fundamental questions remain poorly understood. What do we mean by diversity, and how should we measure diversity in datasets and LLM outputs? How can we collect or select training data and in-context examples that capture greater diversity? When does diversity actually help and which kinds? At generation time, how can we encourage outputs that better reflect the variability of human language? When can synthetic data enhance diversity, and when might it undermine it? Despite growing interest in these questions, our understanding of the role of diversity in NLP is still in its beginning stages. Our main goal is to provide a platform for researchers interested in these topics to get to know each other and share insights and motivations. We plan to open the session with a 15-minute introduction to the topic and why it is important, ending with open questions. The introduction is followed by an informal discussion of these open questions. Participants are welcome to bring their own discussion topics. The session is organized by the DataDivers team (including Dong Nguyen, Anna Wegmann, Esther Ploeger and Cantao Su).

The New Missions for NLP Research theme asks, among others, how to measure progress beyond benchmark scores. The theme description mentions robustness and model deployment in diverse settings as potential new evaluation settings. The theme also directly asks about the consequences of synthetic data feedback loops. Diversity is central to such questions: The diversity of training data can affect how robust models are across settings, the diversity of model outputs determines the diversity of synthetic data used to train future models, and only diverse evaluation data can show whether models work across settings.

### Queer in AI
**Event:** Affinity group lunch (Day 3)  
**Time:** 12:30–14:00  
**Location:** G12  
**Organizer:** Dennis Ulmer

**Description:**  
Queer in AI’s mission is to raise awareness of queer issues in AI/ML, foster a community of queer researchers and celebrate the work of queer scientists. We use “queer” as an umbrella term for people with diverse non-normative sexual orientations, romantic orientations, and/or genders, corresponding to acronyms like LGBTQIA+. We also explicitly include those questioning their identities. It was established by queer scientists in AI with the mission to make the AI community a safe and inclusive place that welcomes, supports, and values LGBTQIA+ people. Queer in AI’s demographic survey reveals that most queer scientists in our community do not feel completely welcome in conferences or their work environments, with the main reasons being a lack of queer community and role models. At EMNLP 2026, we will host a networking lunch. After an initial presentation with more information on Queer in AI, you invite you to socialize with other researchers. Please make sure to bring your own lunch and join the corresponding group in the conference app for more information on the event and a potential Queer in AI after-hours social during one of the evenings of the conference.
