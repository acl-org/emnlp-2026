## Venue

The 2026 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing (EMNLP 2026) will be held at the HUNGEXPO Budapest Congress & Exhibition Center, Hungary's premier convention and exhibition venue.

### HUNGEXPO Budapest Congress & Exhibition Center

Hungexpo Zrt. is Hungary’s leading exhibition and conference organizer and one of the most prominent event venues in Central and Eastern Europe. Founded in 1967, the company has built a strong reputation for hosting major trade fairs, exhibitions, and international conferences, supported by an extensive global network and the backing of French GL events group since 2005. Following a comprehensive redevelopment completed in 2021, the HUNGEXPO Budapest Congress and Exhibition Centre now features modern exhibition halls and a world-class congress center, providing a state-of-the-art venue for exhibitors, event organizers, and visitors while reinforcing Hungexpo’s leadership in the regional events industry.

Located just east of Budapest's city center, HUNGEXPO offers modern meeting facilities, spacious exhibition halls, and dedicated networking areas, providing an ideal setting for our five days of technical sessions, workshops, tutorials, posters, exhibits, and community events.

## Local Information About Budapest
### Language

- **Official language:** Hungarian (*magyar*). English is widely spoken in hotels, restaurants, and tourist areas.
- Learning *"köszönöm"* ([ˈkøsønøm] 'thank you') and *"szia"* ([ˈsijɒ] 'hi/bye') is appreciated.

### Weather

During the EMNLP conference period (assumed November 2025), Budapest's weather is typically:

- **Average temperature:** daytime highs around 12–14 °C (54–57 °F), nighttime lows around 5–7 °C (41–45 °F). Cool, autumnal, occasionally crisp and sunny.
- **Precipitation:** October is moderately wet — expect a few rainy or overcast days; carry a compact umbrella.
- **Practical note:** clocks go back one hour on Sunday, 25 October 2026 (end of daylight saving), so it gets dark by around 17:00. Worth flagging for attendees' travel and jet-lag planning.

### Currency

- **Official currency:** Hungarian Forint (HUF, symbol Ft) — Hungary does not use the euro, although some supermarkets accept euro as well.
- **Exchange rate:** about €1 ≈ 360 HUF and roughly $1 ≈ 310–315 HUF; rates fluctuate, so check before travel.
- **Payment methods:** cards (incl. contactless and mobile pay) are accepted almost everywhere, including on public transport. Some places quote euro but at poor rates, so pay in forint. Prefer bank ATMs (OTP, K&H, Erste, Raiffeisen) and avoid the blue Euronet ATMs, which have poor rates and high fees.

### Power Plugs / Outlets

- **Voltage:** 230 V, 50 Hz.
- **Socket types:** Type C and Type F (Schuko), the standard round two-pin European plug.
- **Recommendation:** visitors from the UK, US, or elsewhere should bring a European adapter; US devices also need a voltage-compatible/dual-voltage charger.

### Clothing

- Dress in layers for autumn: a warm jacket or coat, and a light waterproof layer.
- Sturdy walking shoes for cobblestones. A scarf and umbrella are useful.
- Smart-casual works for most restaurants; the opera or a fine-dining evening warrants something dressier.

### Walking

- Budapest is very walkable, especially central Pest and the Danube embankments, though cobblestones and Buda's hills mean good shoes matter.
- Note that Hungexpo is in District X (Kőbánya), a bit out from the historic centre, so plan public transport rather than walking to the venue from downtown.

### Water

- Tap water is safe, clean, and pleasant to drink throughout Budapest.

## Local Customs (Do's and Don'ts in Hungary)

### Do's

- Greet with *"Jó napot kívánok"* (formal hello) or a simple *"Szia"* informally.
- Say *"Egészségedre!"* (cheers/bless you) when toasting.
- Remove your shoes when entering someone's home.
- Validate/tap your ticket or pass on public transport before or as you board.
- Carry some cash for small vendors, markets, and tips, even though cards are widely accepted.

### Don'ts

- Don't assume prices are in euro — think in forint.
- Don't jaywalk; Hungarians generally wait for the pedestrian signal even on empty streets, and it's enforced.
- Don't expect shops and offices to keep late hours; many close earlier than in some Western capitals.

### Public Transportation Etiquette

- Validate your ticket or activate your pass; inspectors do check and fine.
- Give up priority seats to elderly, disabled, or pregnant passengers.
- Let people exit before boarding, keep bags off seats, and keep voices/phone calls low.
- Escalator convention: stand on the right, walk on the left.

### Tipping

- Tipping around 10% is customary in restaurants and cafés, but check the bill first, as many places now add a service charge (*szervizdíj*); if so, no extra tip is needed.
- Round up for taxis and bar service. It's common to state the total you want to pay when handing over payment rather than leaving coins on the table.

## Things to Do in Budapest

- **Városliget (City Park)** — conveniently near the venue side of town; home to Vajdahunyad Castle and the House of Music Hungary.
- **Margaret Island (Margitsziget)** — car-free island park in the Danube, great for a walk or run.
- **Gellért Hill & the Citadella** — panoramic views over the city.
- **Buda Castle gardens** and the Városmajor.
- **Thermal baths:**
  - *Széchenyi Baths:* the grand neo-baroque complex in City Park, the iconic choice.
  - *Gellért Baths:* art nouveau elegance.
  - *Rudas and Lukács:* historic, more local atmosphere; Rudas has an Ottoman-era dome and a rooftop pool with a river view.
- **Optional day trips along the Danube Bend:** Szentendre, Visegrád, and Esztergom.

### Museums

- Hungarian National Museum
- Museum of Fine Arts and the adjacent Kunsthalle
- Hungarian National Gallery (Buda Castle)
- Ludwig Museum (contemporary)
- House of Terror, and the House of Music Hungary

### Landmarks

- Parliament
- St. Stephen's Basilica
- Fisherman's Bastion
- Dohány Street Synagogue (largest in Europe)

### Shopping

- Váci utca and Fashion Street for central shopping.
- Andrássy út for luxury brands.
- Great Market Hall (Nagyvásárcsarnok) for paprika, Tokaji wine, and souvenirs.
- Malls: Aréna Mall and WestEnd (Aréna is relatively near the venue).
- For design and local crafts, browse the Design Terminal area and District VII boutiques.

### Dining Recommendations

Try Hungarian classics: *gulyás* (goulash soup), *pörkölt*, *lángos*, and chimney cake (*kürtőskalács*).

Notable spots:

- Great Market Hall upstairs stalls
- Menza and Gettó Gulyás (District VII)
- Frici Papa (budget classic)
- The ornate New York Café

Budapest also has a strong fine-dining and Michelin scene if you want a standout dinner. Worth booking ahead.

### Entertainment

- Ruin bars in District VII: Szimpla Kert is the famous one.
- Evening Danube cruise for the illuminated Parliament and bridges.
- Hungarian State Opera House (Andrássy út).
- Live music.
- Thermal-bath "sparties".
- Cafés and bars of the Jewish Quarter.

## Best Transportation to Get Around the City

### From Airport / Train Station to Venue

- From **Budapest Ferenc Liszt International Airport (BUD):** the 100E Airport Express bus runs to Deák Ferenc tér downtown; from there connect via metro M2 toward the venue.
- Alternatively, the 200E bus links the airport to the M3 metro at Kőbánya-Kispest.
- A Bolt or Főtaxi to the Hungexpo/city area is straightforward if you prefer door-to-door.
- **To Hungexpo:** the nearest metro is M2 (red line), Pillangó utca or Puskás Ferenc Stadion, with a short walk or connecting bus/tram to the Albertirsai út entrance. Hungexpo Budapest Congress Center (Entrance), Albertirsai út 10, 1101 Hungary - Gate III (Gate3)

### Transportation to the Venue

- **Metro:** The easiest way to reach Hungexpo by public transportation is via the Budapest Metro M2 (Red Line) to Örs vezér tere Station. From the station, attendees may board the complimentary EMNLP shuttle or enjoy a short walk to the venue. For metro routes, schedules, and fares, visit the Budapest Transport Centre (BKK) website: <https://bkk.hu/en/>
- **Complimentary EMNLP Shuttle:** A complimentary EMNLP shuttle will operate between Örs vezér tere Metro Station (M2 – Red Line) and the Hungexpo. A detailed shuttle schedule will be posted closer to the conference.
- **Uber & Taxi:** Uber, Bolt, licensed taxis, and other rideshare services are readily available throughout Budapest. Recommended drop-off location: Hungexpo Budapest Congress Center – Gate III (Gate 3).

### General Transportation Information

- BKK public transport is excellent: 4 metro lines, extensive trams (the 4/6 and riverside 2 are scenic), trolleybuses, and buses.
- Buy and validate tickets or 24h/72h/weekly passes via the BudapestGO app.
- Contactless bank-card tap-to-ride is available on many lines.
- Bolt is the go-to ride-hailing app.
- For taxis, use Főtaxi (the official airport taxi) or Bolt.
- Avoid unmarked street taxis.

## Practical Information

### Important Notes

- Clocks go back one hour on 25 October 2026 (DST ends).
- EU roaming applies for EU SIMs; others can buy a local prepaid SIM/eSIM (Yettel, Telekom, Vodafone/One).
- Tap water is safe.
- Validate transit tickets.
- Carry a little cash.
- Pay in forint, not euro.
- Standard EU consumer norms; shops often close earlier than in Western Europe.

### In Case of an Emergency

- **112:** single EU emergency number (police, ambulance, fire).
- **Pharmacy:** *gyógyszertár* / *patika*; some are open 24 hours (e.g. near Teréz körút). Look for a green cross sign.
- If medical assistance is needed at the conference, please contact a volunteer or staff member.
